# F4 · The superseded record stays, and keeps its own title

**Applies to:** author.

**What holds.** A superseded record is not deleted, not emptied and not renamed. Its title keeps describing what **it** decided, not what its successor decides; it receives a status marker and a pointer to the successor.

**Why.** The superseded record is the only place where the abandoned solution and its rationale of the time are written down. Deleting it produces exactly the state the concept avoids: a decision without a traceable prehistory. And its own title matters more than it looks: if the old record carried the successor's title, two records with the same name would sit in the index, and citing a decision would be ambiguous.

**Obvious and rejected.** *Move superseded records into an archive directory*: breaks every existing pointer to them, and the chain becomes unreadable as soon as it crosses the directory boundary. *Pull the title up to the new state so the index looks consistent*: creates duplicates in the index and makes citations ambiguous; the index shows standing by other means anyway (F8).

**How you notice it is violated.** Two entries in the index carry the same name, or a pointer to an older decision leads nowhere.
