# F3 · The handover is a deliberate act, and it copies instead of linking

**Applies to:** author.

**What holds.** Nothing gets from the workshop into the product in passing. Whatever is taken over is taken over **with its statement and its reason**, not as a reference to a document of the workshop. The product must be fully understandable without any access to the work repository. Whatever has been taken over is, when needed, generated anew in full from the source and not updated piecemeal.

**Why.** A reference from the product into the workshop is dead for every outsider, and because the product still looks complete, nobody notices. Taking things over with their reason costs writing, and that is the price for the published work standing on its own. And the deliberateness of the handover is the only effective control over what goes out: if you copy in passing, you will at some point also copy what should not go. Generating anew instead of updating keeps the product from contradicting itself: a projection cannot contradict itself, it can at most lag behind as a whole, and between two generations the product is knowingly out of date, which is bearable as long as nothing in it claims the opposite.

**Considered and rejected.** *Linking or including workshop documents symbolically*: they are not reachable for the reader; the link looks like a solution and is none. *Simply publishing the workshop documents along with the product*: then the separation is lifted (F1). *Synchronizing automatically*: takes away exactly the decision that gives the handover its value.

**How you notice it is violated.** A document in the product refers to one that is only in the workshop, or somebody cannot say when and why something was published.
