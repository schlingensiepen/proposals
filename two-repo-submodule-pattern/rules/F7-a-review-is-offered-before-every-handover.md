# F7 · A review is offered before every handover

**Applies to:** agent · author.

**What holds.** Before every handover into the product the agent **offers a full review, emphatically**. Alternatively it asks once whether this should always be so; if the answer is yes, that counts as a standing approval, which can be switched off in the individual case. The review follows the **two-models rule**: two independent reviewers of different models, neither of them the model that built. It runs as a chain of reviewing, incorporating and reviewing again until it is settled. If it is not settled after at most three rounds, it ends with a **convergence report to the human**, never with a silent approval. One fixed question belongs in every review: does what is taken over contain names, access paths or statements about third parties? For the self-contained documents beyond the handover, the review includes the comprehension check by a reader without inside knowledge, as imported from `proven-from-outside`: a fresh agent without access to the workshop plays the target reader, retells the content, walks through it and lists the places where it had to guess.

**Why.** The deliberateness of the handover (F3) is the only control over what goes out, and a deliberate act without a check depends on the attention of the moment. An offer with emphasis, not a gate, keeps the decision with the human and still makes the review the normal case. A reviewer of the same model as the builder shares its blind spots; two reviewers of different models find what each alone misses. The comprehension check tests exactly the property that counts beyond the handover: the document carries without the source. The limit of three rounds keeps a chain that does not converge from running on; then the human has to decide, and a silent approval would hide exactly that.

**Considered and rejected.** *A review as a gate that blocks the handover*: takes the decision away from the human instead of supporting it. *A single reviewer, or the building model reviewing its own work*: shares the blind spots of what is under review. *Reviewing until it is settled, without a limit*: a chain that does not converge does not end by itself. *Approving silently once the rounds run out*: pretends to a result that was not reached. *A checklist of its own instead of a fixed question in the review*: names, access paths and statements about third parties are one question among those a reviewer asks anyway, not a second procedure.

**How you notice it is violated.** Something reached the product without a review having been offered; a review was carried out by the model that built; or a review ended neither settled nor with a convergence report, and nobody can say who approved the handover.
