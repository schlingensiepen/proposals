# Evidence

## Origin

The concept comes from the author's own work with an AI agent on a draft-filing service; the cases below come from that collaboration. Neither repository of that service is publicly accessible, so the cases are told here in full rather than linked.

## Applied in the author's project

For the draft-filing service there are two repositories: one for the genesis (concept, short specification, decision log, plans, session states) and one for the product. The genesis is not an accessory here: the decision log is actually used as memory, above all by agents that continue working on the project. That is exactly the reason it must not be smoothed.

## The tool limit from which F5 arose

In practice the case occurred that all work on the repositories ran through an interface that can create and change files, but not submodule references, because these are not file content but an entry type of their own in the directory structure. Creating the submodule anyway and advancing the reference by hand was considered; it was rejected, because a reference that does not move along pretends to a precision it does not have. The choice was two independent roots with an ignore rule for the embedded directory, and the proposal for it came from the author once the limit had been named. The wording of F5 comes from this episode, including the reason it is not a makeshift.

## The announcement rule of F6

It arose from the same collaboration: one repository was assigned to the agent as its free workshop, the other it was allowed to touch only with prior announcement. The rule has held; a case in which it would have slowed things down needlessly has not occurred.

## State of play

Long-term operating experience is missing. The question of how well the redundancy from F3 can be maintained over time, open in earlier drafts, is answered by F3 itself: it is not maintained but generated anew in full from the source. As far as is known today, the submodule form (F4) was considered in the same project and not implemented, in favor of F5.

## Answers from a second project

A second project that applies the separation reported back on the open points of this draft. It generates the documents beyond the handover anew from the knowledge base of its workshop at fixed points instead of updating them by hand; the trigger is the release of a state for delivery, or a request. Before every handover its agent offers a review by two reviewers of other models, with at most three rounds and a convergence report to the human, and a comprehension check by an agent without access to the workshop. Every release there names the state and the purpose, is recorded as a decision and marked with a tag in the product. From the same project comes the usual allocation of workshop and product in the concept. These are the origins of the regeneration sentence in F3, of F7, of F8 and of the usual allocation. The project's repository is not publicly accessible.
