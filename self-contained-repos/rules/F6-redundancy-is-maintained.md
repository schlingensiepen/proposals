# F6 · Redundancy is maintained, not avoided

**Applies to:** writer · maintainer.

**What holds.** Because knowledge is deliberately in the repository more than once, every change of content comes with the question which other places it touches, and the answer belongs in the same change. For the most important pairings (discussion paper and instruction, instruction and checklist, document and register entry) this is written down explicitly instead of being left to memory.

**Why.** Redundancy is the counterpart of self-containment, and it has a price: two places can drift apart. The difference between redundancy that holds and lazy redundancy is exactly this maintenance. If you do not organize it, you get the worst of both worlds: copies that drift apart without anybody noticing, because each looks plausible on its own.

**Considered and rejected.** *Tools that keep copies in sync*: work for text snippets, not for knowledge that has to appear in different forms (command versus reasoning). *Avoiding redundancy after all and linking again*: then F1 falls. *Trusting in care*: care without named pairings is chance.

**How you notice it is violated.** Two places in the repository say the same thing differently, and nobody knows which one is right.
