# F4 · Instructions are self-contained, even toward their own repository

**Applies to:** writer.

**What holds.** An instruction that can be passed on on its own is self-contained even toward the other files of the repository: every rule and every check question is written out in it. References to numbers or sections of other documents appear only as a provenance note in parentheses and must not be needed in order to follow it.

**Why.** This rule arose from a concrete objection: a checklist that hangs its points on numbers of another document forces anyone who carries it out to load the other document in full after all; then the separate file is pointless, and the whole effort of separating it was wasted. The provenance note remains useful nevertheless, but for a different purpose: it serves the maintainer when reconciling, not the person carrying out the work.

**Considered and rejected.** *A short checklist with references, the long version in the main document*: saves space and costs usability. *Leaving out the instruction entirely and maintaining only the main document*: forces everyone who carries out a task to read a discussion paper in order to get an instruction for action. *Generating it automatically from the main document*: was considered; but the instruction has a different form from the reasoning (command instead of argument), and a mechanical derivation produces either commands that argue or arguments that command.

**How you notice it is violated.** Anyone who wants to follow the instruction has to open a second document.
