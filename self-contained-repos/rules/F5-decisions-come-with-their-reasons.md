# F5 · Decisions come into the document with their reasons

**Applies to:** writer.

**What holds.** Where a document relies on a decision, **statement and reasons** are taken over, and, where it carries the matter, also the alternatives that were considered and rejected, each with the reason for rejecting it. A link to the decision log is a maintenance note, never a prerequisite for understanding. Where the document is generated from the source that holds the decision (F7), what was brought in is, when needed, generated anew in full from that source and not updated piecemeal.

**Why.** The reasons are the most expensive part of a decision and the first to get lost. Without them the decision is given up at the first resistance, because nobody knows any more what it prevents. And the rejected alternatives are even more expensive when they are missing: whoever does not read them proposes the rejected solution again, friendly, plausible, and without anybody remembering the reason. A decision log still makes sense: it is the history with date and course. It is just not the place where the work happens.

**Obvious and rejected.** *Only linking ("see decision 12")*: saves writing; it is exactly the coupling that F1 forbids. *Taking over only the statement and leaving out the reasons*: produces rules that look arbitrary and are treated accordingly. *Putting the log into the repository and leaving it at that*: the log is ordered chronologically, the working document by subject; whoever searches in the log searches for a long time.

**How you notice it is violated.** A rejected solution is proposed again; or somebody asks "why, actually?" and nobody can answer without fetching another document.
